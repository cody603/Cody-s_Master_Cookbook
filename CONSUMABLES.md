# Consumables — the repeat-buy log

**This is not the cookbook, and it isn't a recipe list.** It is the short list of things this house buys over and over on its own rhythm — drinks, milk, paper goods, cleaning supplies — plus **when each was bought**, so a chat session can say *"it's been a week since the Zevia, are you low?"* instead of waiting to be told.

Cody, 2026-09-14: *"I'd like you to say — hey, it's been a week, you know, and you've cooked this and this and this. Are you low on Zevia? … I think we're missing drinks and the necessity of drinks in our system. Obviously that's kind of separate than the actual cookbook."* And later the same day: *"there is going to be kind of repeat items like milk… miscellaneous items, toilet paper, paper towels, cleaning supplies, trash bags — all the time we're buying that and putting it on our order… Obviously this does not go into the cookbook or any kind of cooking methods. It just goes into the grocery planning system… maybe you can learn the behavior. Oh, you bought trash bags. And then, oh, you bought trash bags a week later or two weeks later… and I think you can also set a frequency too."*

## How it works

- **A chat session reads this file when planning a week or building a grocery list**, and **asks about everything that's due in one sentence** — *"trash bags, paper towels and the Zevia are all about due — want any of those?"* One question, one answer. Never one item at a time; Cody's rule is that the planning phase must not bottleneck on questions.
- **Chat may write to this file directly**, with no approval needed: stamping a purchase date, or adding an item someone says they buy regularly. It's a shopping log, not the book. *(Anything that would change a **recipe** still goes through `PROPOSED-REVISIONS.md`.)*
- **Stamp the date when a shopping run actually happens** — when the cart is filled or the list is handed off — not when the item is merely discussed. **Keep the dates, newest first**; that history is how the interval gets learned.
- **Two kinds of interval.** **Set** — Cody or whoever buys it names the rhythm, and that's the number. **Learned** — nobody has said, so the interval is *unknown* until there are two or three purchase dates to average; then write the observed gap and mark it *learned*. A learned interval never overrides a set one. Don't invent either kind.
- **"Due" means the last purchase is older than the interval.** An item with no interval yet and no date yet is never due — it's only added when someone asks for it, and that first date starts the clock.

## What does not go here

- **Ingredients for a specific dish** — those come off that recipe's own Grocery Shopping List when the dish is planned.
- **Non-recipe foods that aren't on a clock** — fruit, snacks, sandwich fixings. Those are `HOUSEHOLD-STAPLES.md`.
- **Anything about a recipe** — quantities, methods, ratings. Those are `PROPOSED-REVISIONS.md`.
- **What got cooked** — `COOKING-LOG.md`.
- **The printed cookbook.** Nothing in this file will ever appear in it.

## An honest limit

**Nothing here can reach out to anyone.** A chat session has no background process and cannot send a reminder on its own. **The check happens when someone starts a conversation** — planning a week, building a list, asking what's for dinner. That is when this file gets read and the question gets asked. If nobody opens a chat for three weeks, nobody gets asked about the Zevia.
