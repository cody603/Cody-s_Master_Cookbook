# Cooking Log — what got cooked, and when

**This is not the cookbook.** It is the running record of which dishes this house actually made, on which night, and how they went — so a chat session can say *"you've cooked this and this and this this week"*, so the repeat-buy log in `CONSUMABLES.md` has something to reason from, and so the printed cookbook one day knows which dishes are the real regulars.

Cody, 2026-09-14: *"I would like you to log dates on when we cook things… like a cooking date log. If I say, hey, I'm gonna eat this tonight, we're wanting to cook this tonight or whatever, you can go ahead and log it, and that would be just fine. And we'll just have to have an ongoing conversation because I may or may not end up cooking that."*

## How it works

- **Chat writes this file directly — no approval needed.** It's a diary, not the book.
- **Log it the moment someone says it's for tonight**, marked **provisional**. Plans slip — *"we were supposed to cook lemon chicken soup yesterday, we were too tired and we didn't get the ingredients."* So a row goes in when the plan is spoken, and gets **confirmed**, **skipped**, or **moved** the next time it comes up. Don't wait to be told twice; don't ask about it either — the next conversation that mentions that night settles it.
- **A verdict is optional and short.** *Phenomenal.* *Too salty.* *Kids didn't touch it.* Anything longer than a sentence that would change the recipe is a revision request in `PROPOSED-REVISIONS.md`, not a log note.
- **One row per night, every dish on it**, linked by anchor so the planner can find it. Who cooked, if it was said.
- **Newest first.**

## What does not go here

- **Recipe changes** — `PROPOSED-REVISIONS.md`.
- **What was bought** — `CONSUMABLES.md` keeps the purchase dates.
- **The plan for the week before it's cooked** — that lives in the conversation and the calendar; a row lands here only when a night is named as *tonight* or has happened.
